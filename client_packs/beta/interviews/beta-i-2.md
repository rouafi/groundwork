Interviewer: You look after the mid-sized plants. Do you write like the enterprise team?

Regional manager: No, and that is on purpose. With mid-sized customers we write casually and directly. First names, short sentences, no corporate phrasing. These are plant managers who run a site with forty people, they read email on their phone between two machines. A formal three-paragraph email from us just does not get read.

Interviewer: So the formal default does not apply to them?

Regional manager: Not for mid-sized accounts, no. Casual, but still precise. Casual does not mean vague.

Interviewer: What do your customers ask about most?

Regional manager: Parts. When a line is down, they want to know when the part arrives. I tell them spare parts arrive next day anywhere in the world, because that is what keeps them calm on the phone. Most of the time it is true.

Interviewer: How do you involve the technical side?

Regional manager: Every email that touches a technical question goes to the assigned technician lead in copy. Always. The plant manager will forward it to them anyway, and if they are in copy from the start, we save a day.

Interviewer: What about prices with your customers?

Regional manager: For mid-sized customers I am fine giving the list price in the email when they ask, especially for training. Technician Training is 9600 dollars and they want to know that before they book a meeting. A formal quote follows if they go ahead.

Interviewer: Anything about tone when things go wrong?

Regional manager: Own it. If our technician was late, say it, apologize once, and give the new time. Do not hide behind "operational constraints".

Interviewer: What would you tell someone joining your region?

Regional manager: Visit the plants. Then write to people like you have met them, because you have.
