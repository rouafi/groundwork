Interviewer: Your team delivers audits and training. What do you put in writing to customers?

Service delivery lead: Deadlines, carefully. For an On-site Audit, we promise the report is delivered within 10 business days after the visit, and we put that in the confirmation email. We never promise a shorter time, even when the auditor thinks he can do it faster.

Interviewer: How about training?

Service delivery lead: Technician Training includes certification, up to twelve technicians per session. The confirmation email lists who is enrolled and says certification is included. If they want to send fourteen people, that is a second session, and we say so clearly instead of squeezing them in.

Interviewer: How do you write internally?

Service delivery lead: Internal handoffs between the sales team and my team always include the ticket number and the site name in the subject line. We run hundreds of visits a year. Without the site name, an internal email is useless.

Interviewer: Are there things your team should never write to a customer?

Service delivery lead: Safety conclusions before the report is final. An auditor might see something worrying on site, but nothing about safety goes in an email until the report is reviewed. A preliminary remark in writing can end up in a legal file.

Interviewer: Do you adapt to who reads the email?

Service delivery lead: For the technical evaluators, the maintenance engineers, we attach the checklist we use on site and name the standards we audit against. They want to prepare. For the economic buyers we summarize in three lines: what we found, the risk, the recommended next step.

Interviewer: Prices?

Service delivery lead: I leave prices to sales. If a customer asks me, I forward the question to their account owner the same day, I do not answer it myself.

Interviewer: Last thing?

Service delivery lead: Confirm everything in writing after a phone call. Date, scope, names. It protects the customer and it protects us.
