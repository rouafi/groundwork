# Tone rubric: Beta (synthetic client)

Score the tone of the email as the contact would experience it, from 1 to 5. Facts and prices are checked elsewhere: do not grade them.

What good looks like, by audience (from the client's own rules):
- **Enterprise customers:** formal. Full name and title in the greeting, no emojis, no exclamation marks, a proper signature. Written so it can be forwarded to a director without editing.
- **Mid-sized customers:** casual and direct. First names, short sentences, no corporate phrasing, still precise.
- **The economic buyer:** three lines at most: what we found, the risk, the recommended next step.
- **Technical evaluators:** precise, names the standards and what to prepare.
- **When something went wrong:** say what happened, apologize once, give the new time, and do not hide behind "operational constraints".
- **Internal handoffs:** ticket number and site name in the subject line, only the facts.

Scale:
- **5**: fits the audience and the channel on every point above, and reads like an experienced service manager wrote it.
- **4**: fits the audience, with one small slip (one casual phrase in a formal email, one sentence too many).
- **3**: acceptable but generic or slightly off for the audience (rambling, unstructured, too warm or too cold).
- **2**: clearly off: the wrong register for the audience, emojis or exclamation marks to an enterprise, or several slips at once.
- **1**: unusable: curt or rude, blames the customer, or ignores the audience entirely.
