Interviewer: Your team sits between sales, support and the product. How do you write?

Customer success lead: Two very different ways. Between colleagues, internally, we write bullet points and skip the formalities. No greetings, no sign-off, just what happened, what we need and by when. If a message to a colleague needs scrolling, it should have been a ticket.

Interviewer: And to customers?

Customer success lead: To customers we are careful. The big one: we never promise dates for features on the roadmap. Product changes priorities, and a date in an email becomes a commitment the customer will quote back at us. We say "it is on the roadmap" or nothing.

Interviewer: Does it depend on who reads the email?

Customer success lead: Yes. Technical evaluators, the people testing the integration, want precision. With them we link the exact page of the API documentation and give real field names. No hand-waving, no "it should work". If we are not sure, we say we are checking.

Interviewer: What about prices? Do you talk about them?

Customer success lead: Rarely, but when we do, we use the catalog list price. Same rule as sales: the price in the email is the price in the catalog, word for word. If a customer asks about a discount, I hand over to their account owner.

Interviewer: How do you keep emails consistent across the team?

Customer success lead: We reuse a few templates for onboarding. The Onboarding Pack includes two training sessions, and our welcome email says exactly that, nothing more. People used to promise unlimited training to be nice, and then we could not deliver.

Interviewer: Anything about tone that is specific to your customers?

Customer success lead: Our customers are mostly small and mid-sized firms, so we avoid jargon. Write like you would explain it to a smart colleague from another department. Short sentences, active voice.

Interviewer: What do you wish sales did differently?

Customer success lead: Write down what they promised. Half of my onboarding calls start with "but the salesperson said". If it is not in the CRM, I cannot honor it.
