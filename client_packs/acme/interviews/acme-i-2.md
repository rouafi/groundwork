Interviewer: You handle existing customers. How is writing to them different from writing to prospects?

Account manager: The relationship is already there, so I am more direct. But the first rule I follow is speed. I reply to every customer email within one business day, even if the answer is "I am checking and will come back on Thursday". Silence is what makes people churn.

Interviewer: Do you adapt the message to who is reading?

Account manager: A lot. When I write to the person who signs the contract, the economic buyer, I lead with cost of ownership and the time their team saves, not with features. They do not care about a new chart type. They care that the report that took two days now takes an hour.

Interviewer: And renewals?

Account manager: Renewals are where I disagree a bit with sales. For mid-sized customers, I put a ten percent renewal discount directly in the renewal email when they renew for two years. It closes faster, and it is our standard offer, so I do not see the risk. Sales prefers to keep it on the phone, but for renewals writing it down saves everyone a meeting.

Interviewer: What do customers ask you most?

Account manager: Security, more and more. I always tell Pro customers that single sign-on is included, because that is the first question their IT team asks. It unblocks the deal.

Interviewer: How do you handle bad news, like an outage?

Account manager: Plain and early. Say what happened, what it affected, and when the next update comes. No marketing words, no "we are thrilled". And I never blame the customer's setup in writing, even when it is their setup.

Interviewer: Any habits around the CRM?

Account manager: I log every promise I make in the CRM on the same day. If I told someone they get two training sessions, it goes in the notes, because the next person on the account needs to know what was promised.

Interviewer: Last question. What would you tell a new account manager?

Account manager: Read the last three emails in the thread before you write. Half the mistakes I see are people answering a question that was already answered, or contradicting what a colleague promised last month.
